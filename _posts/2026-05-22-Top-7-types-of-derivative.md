## 6. partial

I can look at how one location and compare how it changed over time: I could call this history or I can look at a fixed point in time in the past and compare different places:  I could call this geography?. What about something like the Roman empire which spread over time: I could follow the path of the a Roman legion as it moves through space and time and measure something along its path: this would be called a directional derivative.

## 5. material

Imagine a bear sitting on the bank of a river with a fishing rod. It looks at the river and wonders how it could apply partial derivatives. It could take a screenshot in time and look at how the flow of the river changed from point to point. It could also look at a point in the flow and see how the flow changes over time (say by attaching a detector to the river bed). However this is only one perspective (called the Eulerian one). If I were a fish being carried along in the flow, hoping to avoid the bear's fishing rod, (whaling, fishing, crabbing are the only examples I can think of where turning an animal's name into a verb means to hunt that animal) I could also measure how the flow was changing: am I speeding up, changing direction? And perhaps this is a more natural way to phrase the laws of fluid mechanics. A point that is carried along in the flow is affected by its neighbouring points which are carried along in the same flow. This idea is encapsulated by the material derivative. I talk about this in more depth in part one of my Navier-Stokes post (link this).

## 4. Weak

I want to broaden the space of functions in which I search for solutions. I can integrate any integrable function (duh) but I can only differentiate some functions: a function like the absolute value has that pesky discontinuity in its derivative around zero: but integration doesn't care. Moreover I can multiply it by any smooth function and integrate and this will still make perfect sense. But multiplying functions and integrating is reminiscent of integration by parts.
