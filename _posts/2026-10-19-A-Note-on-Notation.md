---
Title: A Note on Notation
Date: 2026-10-19
---

Terence Tao has written extensively about the way that good notation can "relieve the brain of all unnecessary work" and "set it free to concentrate on more advanced problems" (<a href="https://terrytao.wordpress.com/advice-on-writing-papers/use-good-notation/">here in his blog</a> and <a href="https://mathoverflow.net/questions/366070/what-are-the-benefits-of-writing-vector-inner-products-as-langle-u-v-rangle">here in an answer on mathoverflow</a>). He argues that the relationship between symbols should respect the relationship between the objects they represent: $u,v,w$ might be vectors in $V$ but if $V=U\oplus W$ then I sure hope $u\in U$ and $w\in W$. In a similar respect, Nick Higham said that a symmetric matrix should be represented by a symmetric letter like $A$ or $M$. While I'm not so convinced by this, I think that only commutative operations should have symmetric symbols. Good examples of this are: $x + y, x\times y, A \cap B, A \cup B$. There's a reason I'll always use $A\setminus B$ instead of $A - B$. When the direction matters, the symbol should reflect this, not the order of writing: $x\le y$ and $A \subset B$ are equivalent to $y\ge x$ and  $B \supset A$ however $x \vert y$ doesn't indicate a direction. Some other offenders are $x - y$, $dx\wedge dy$ and $a\propto ka$. Worst of all, as 3B1B has pointed out, we can convey the same piece of information in three different way: $2^3 =8$, ${} \sqrt[3]{8} = 2 {}$, $\log_{2}(8) = 3$. 

What made me think about this is the lack of a symbol which I hope exists (please put me out of my misery if it already does). We don't write $A\cup B = B$ or $A\cap B = A$ to say that $A$ is a subset of $B$. While logically correct, we invented a symbol for this common scenario. I would argue that stating whether two sets are disjoint occurs frequently enough to deserve a symbol. In both cases too! If $E\cap F = \emptyset$ then $\mu(E\cup F) = \mu(E) + \mu(F)$ and if $U_i \cap U_j \neq \emptyset$ then the transition map $\phi_{ij}$ should be smooth. I propose a symmetric symbol to denote whether two sets intersect non-trivially: the best I've found is $\between$ for 'non-disjoint' but I am open to suggestions!
